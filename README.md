## 2. Quantum Architecture & Mechanics

* **Core Quantum Principles Used:**  
  `Superposition (H gate) and measurement collapse.`

* **How the Quantum Circuit is Integrated:**  
  `Two qubits each get a Hadamard gate, giving an equal superposition of 00, 01, 10 and 11. One measurement collapses it to one outcome: 00 = stone, 01 = paper, 10 = scissors. If 11 is measured it has no meaning, so the circuit is measured again. This keeps each move at exactly 1/3.`

- **Circuit Details:**
  * **Number of Qubits:** `2`
  * **Key Gates Used:** `H, Measurement`
  * **Primitives Used:** `StatevectorSampler`
  * **Other Technologies Used:** `tkinter for UI (Qiskit powers the quantum logic)`

```python
qc = QuantumCircuit(2, 2)
qc.h(0)
qc.h(1)
qc.measure([0, 1], [0, 1])
````

## 6. Performance, Results & Limitations

* **Key Results & Observations:**  
  `On simulating or playing the game 1500 times. There is almost equal probability in choice of computer response for each input. `

* **Critical Reflection on Limitations:**  
  `On a simulator the outcome is ideal. On real NISQ hardware, readout errors and gate noise could bias the three moves slightly. Using 2 qubits and rejecting 11 wastes 25% of measurements. This game does not need quantum hardware; it is a demonstration.`
